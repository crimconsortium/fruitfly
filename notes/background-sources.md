## 1. Ashby, open-access availability of criminological research (JCJE)

### Citation

- Exact title: "The Open-Access Availability of Criminological Research to Practitioners and Policy Makers" — https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10113235/
- Author: Matthew P J Ashby, Lecturer in crime science, Jill Dando Institute of Security and Crime Science, University College London; ORCID 0000-0003-4201-9239 — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Journal: *Journal of Criminal Justice Education*, volume 32, issue 1, pages 1–21 — https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10113235/
- DOI: 10.1080/10511253.2020.1838588 — https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10113235/
- Year: UCL Discovery and the journal issue give 2021; the article was published online 2020 and is often cited as 2020 (Ashby's own site lists 2020; Taylor & Francis "by MPJ Ashby · 2020") — https://lesscrime.info/publication/criminology-oa/ and https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10511253.2020.1838588
- Publisher: Taylor & Francis — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Accepted-manuscript version date: 09 October 2020 — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf

### Sample

- 12,541 articles analysed via Unpaywall — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Drawn from 97 criminology and related journals — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Publication window: January 2017 through December 2019 — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Source list came from CrimPapers, which discovered 13,469 articles; the 12,541 matched articles = 93.1% of 2017–2019 CrimPapers articles — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Unmatched 6.9% breaks down as 2.4% journals with no DOIs, 4.3% DOI not identified, 0.2% DOI absent from Unpaywall — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Unpaywall data snapshot date: 4 March 2020 — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf

### Headline findings (as stated)

- Abstract, verbatim: "Overall 22% of research was available to non-subscribers, about half that found in other disciplines, even though authors had the right to make articles open without payment in at least 95% of cases." — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Precise figure in the body: 2,814 articles (22.4%) were available without a subscription via bronze, gold, or green OA; 9,727 articles (77.6%) were not — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Paper's own framing: "Currently more than three quarters of criminological research is hidden from public view." — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Cross-discipline comparison: 22.4% is "slightly less than half" the 44.7% OA rate Piwowar et al. (2018) found for 2015 articles (27.9% across their whole random 100,000-article sample) — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Counterfactual: ~9,100 articles published 2017–2019 could have been freely available but were not — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Journal policies permitted immediate preprint posting for 99% of articles and postprints for 95% — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf

### Breakdown by OA type

- Green: 10.5% — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Bronze: 6.3% — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Gold: 5.6% (combines gold in hybrid journals and in pure OA journals; no separate hybrid figure is reported) — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Green OA detail: 1,321 green articles across 267 different websites; 51% available as preprints, 49% as postprints, 15% as the final published version, 13% in multiple versions, 61% in some peer-reviewed version — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Where green copies sat: 51% in UK university institutional repositories, 17% Semantic Scholar, 15% PubMed Central/Europe PMC, 11% US university repositories — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Unpaywall does not index ResearchGate/Academia.edu, so no figure is given for academic social networks — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf

### Breakdown by publisher and journal

- Every publisher's OA share fell below the Piwowar et al. cross-discipline benchmark — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Taylor & Francis — the largest criminology publisher by both articles and journals — had the lowest proportion of OA articles — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Springer Nature: 18% gold OA vs a weighted mean of 10% gold for other publishers — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- All 20 leading criminology journals fell below the cross-discipline benchmark; in almost all of them more than three quarters of articles were subscription-only — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Best and worst named leading journals: *Psychology of Violence* 36% OA; *Justice Quarterly* 9% OA ("only about 1 in 12 articles") — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf

### Breakdown by author country

- United States 12% OA; Canada 12% OA — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- United Kingdom: 39% green OA; Netherlands: 36% gold OA and a majority of Dutch-authored articles were OA overall — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- More than half of papers in the sample had at least one US author — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf

### On the "what practitioners/policymakers could read" number — caution

- **The paper does not report a separate percentage for practitioners/policymakers.** The 22% / 22.4% "available to non-subscribers" figure *is* the practitioner-and-policymaker-access figure; the framing of who is disadvantaged (researchers in the global south, policy makers, practitioners without subscriptions) comes from the abstract — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- Ashby's own plain-language summary uses the same single number: "Overall 22% of research was available to non-subscribers" — https://lesscrime.info/publication/criminology-oa/

### Is the article itself open access?

- The publisher landing page is an abstract-only page ("/doi/abs/"), i.e. the version of record is behind Taylor & Francis subscription access — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10511253.2020.1838588
- **UNVERIFIED:** I did not successfully load the Taylor & Francis full-text page to confirm the paywall status of the version of record directly (the publisher page blocked retrieval beyond the abstract listing).
- A free, openly downloadable accepted manuscript (author accepted version, identical to published text apart from publisher formatting) is available in two places a reader can open:
  - UCL Discovery record with PDF (891 kB) — https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10113235/
  - Direct PDF on Ashby's site — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf
- A SocArXiv preprint record also exists — https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv/wnq7h_v1 (page itself blocked automated retrieval; existence confirmed via search listing)
- Nice irony to note carefully: a study finding that 77.6% of criminology research is subscription-only was itself published in a Taylor & Francis journal — the publisher the paper identifies as having the lowest OA share — with the free copy available only as a self-archived green postprint — https://lesscrime.info/files/open_access_to_criminology_postprint.pdf and https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10113235/

## 2. The MaleCNS connectome (2026)

### The paper

- Peer-reviewed title (Cell/PubMed wording): "Sexual dimorphism in the complete *Drosophila* male central nervous system connectome" — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42691995/
- Preprint and institutional press wording of the title differs slightly: "Sexual dimorphism in the complete connectome of the *Drosophila* male central nervous system" — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/ and https://www.zoo.cam.ac.uk/news/world-first-male-fly-brain-map-helps-unlock-secret-workings-mind
- Venue: *Cell*, volume 189, issue 18, pages 5504–5526.e15, published 3 September 2026 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42691995/
- DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.015; PMID 42691995 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42691995/
- Publisher full-text URL: https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00942-6 (listed in search results; the page blocked automated retrieval on my attempt)
- First author Stuart Berg; senior/last author Gregory S X E Jefferis; ~100 co-authors including Michał Januszewski and Viren Jain (Google), Gerald M Rubin, Harald F Hess, Gwyneth M Card, Michael B Reiser, Carlos Ribeiro, Scott Waddell — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42691995/
- Preprint: bioRxiv 2025.10.09.680999, version 2 posted 30 October 2025, DOI 10.1101/2025.10.09.680999, PMCID PMC12636603, PMID 41279223 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/
- bioRxiv v1 landing page — https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.09.680999v1
- Free full text of the preprint (readable now) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/

### Who

- Institutions: FlyEM Project Team, HHMI Janelia Research Campus; University of Cambridge Department of Zoology (Drosophila Connectomics Group); MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology (Jefferis Group); Connectomics group at Google Research — https://male-cns.janelia.org/ and https://www.janelia.org/project-team/flyem/male-cns-connectome
- Cambridge press release adds Champalimaud Foundation as a collaborating institution and names Greg Jefferis as senior author — https://www.zoo.cam.ac.uk/news/world-first-male-fly-brain-map-helps-unlock-secret-workings-mind
- Google's own framing: "We partnered with HHMI Janelia and collaborators to publish a complete map of the male fruit fly's brain and central nervous system, creating the largest brain map to date," posted 3 September 2026 by Michał Januszewski and Viren Jain — https://research.google/blog/a-connectomics-milestone-mapping-the-complete-male-fruit-fly-brain/
- Janelia's claim: "the first finished connectome of an entire male *Drosophila* central nervous system, encompassing the central brain, optic lobes … and ventral nerve cord," with an intact neck connective and the first example of a male brain — https://www.janelia.org/project-team/flyem/male-cns-connectome

### Counts (numbers differ between preprint and published version — use with care)

- Cell/PubMed abstract: "This contains 166,700 neurons spanning the brain and nerve cord" — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42691995/
- Cell/PubMed: 11,710 neuron types, of which 8,069 isomorphic, 138 dimorphic, 289 male-specific, 71 female-specific — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42691995/
- Preprint v2: 166,691 neurons proofread and annotated across the CNS; 166,391 neurons in the connectome graph; 11,691 unique cell types — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/
- bioRxiv v1 abstract text cited variously as 166,696 and 166,691 neurons in search snippets — https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.09.680999v1
- **Synapse counts, preprint v2 (primary):** 46 million automatically detected presynapses connected to 312 million postsynaptic densities; the connectome graph has 25.6 million edges — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/
- **DISCREPANCY:** Google's blog says "over 166,000 neurons and 125 million synaptic connections," a synapse figure that matches neither the preprint's 46M presynapses / 312M PSDs nor its 25.6M graph edges. Most press coverage repeated Google's "125 million." I could not reconcile it against the paper because the Cell full text would not load — treat "125 million synapses" as the Google-blog number, not a verified paper number — https://research.google/blog/a-connectomics-milestone-mapping-the-complete-male-fruit-fly-brain/
- Other preprint QC numbers: synapse prediction average precision 0.82 / recall 0.81; 94% presynaptic and 42% postsynaptic completion in neuropils; 40.1% of connections have both partners in proofread neurons; 141,780 neuron-associated nuclei detected, 98.9% part of a proofread neuron — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/
- Specimen and imaging: single adult male sample Z0720-07m (Canton S G1 × w1118 cross, five-day-old males), imaged 13 months on 7 enhanced FIB-SEM systems, 160 teravoxels at 8×8×8 nm isotropic, 0.054 mm³ of tissue, 66 hot-knife slabs — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/
- Dimorphism headline figures: 262 sex-specific and 114 sexually dimorphic cell types, comprising 4.8% of the central brain (Janelia page) — https://www.janelia.org/project-team/flyem/male-cns-connectome
- Cell full text (via search snippet): "In the male central brain … 3.4% of neurons are male-specific, and 1.4% are sexually dimorphic" — https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(26)00942-6

### Dataset version name and license

- **"MaleCNS v1.0" is a real, published version name.** The project news feed reads: "2026-06-08 — MaleCNS v1.0 released!" and "2026-09-03 — MaleCNS paper published!" — https://male-cns.janelia.org/
- Earlier release: "2025-10-03 — MaleCNS v0.9 released!"; "2025-10-30 — Preprint v2 available on bioRxiv"; "2025-11-07 — NeuronBridge now provides matches for the MaleCNS" — https://male-cns.janelia.org/
- The Cambridge connectomics group's ecosystem review lists the neuPrint dataset identifier as `malecns:v1.0` — https://flyconnecto.me/2026/09/04/the-adult-drosophila-connectome-ecosystem/
- License: the FlyEM Male CNS dataset is licensed CC-BY — https://www.janelia.org/project-team/flyem/male-cns-connectome and https://male-cns.janelia.org/
- **Caution on the license claim:** CC-BY is stated for the *dataset* on the Janelia pages. Separately, the manuscript carries CC BY licensing via funder policy: "For the purpose of open access, the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology has applied a CC BY public copyright licence to any Author Accepted Manuscript version arising," plus HHMI's CC BY 4.0 policy — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/
- **UNVERIFIED:** whether the *Cell* version of record itself is gold open access on cell.com (page would not load); the author accepted manuscript is CC BY and the preprint is freely readable at PMC — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12636603/
- **DISCREPANCY to flag:** dataset v1.0 was released 8 June 2026, three months *before* the 3 September 2026 paper — https://male-cns.janelia.org/. Much of the popular coverage says the data was "released September 3, 2026" (e.g. Know Your Meme) — https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/fly-brain-simulations-fruit-fly-brain-mapped. ### Things to treat with suspicion
